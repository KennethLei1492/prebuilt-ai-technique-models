_Used by: answer_verification_model/._  Dossier: `docs/research/answer-verification.json` (final). Back to the [index](../RESEARCH_BIBLIOGRAPHY.md).

## Summary

Answer verification is the pipeline stage that decides whether a candidate output (answer, plan, code, spec edit, incident summary) is correct, grounded and safe to release, and if not, what to do about it: select a better candidate, repair it with evidence, abstain, or escalate. The literature converges on four families of signal. (1) Agreement across samples: Self-Consistency (Wang et al., ICLR 2023) shows majority vote over ~40 sampled reasoning paths adds +17.9 points on GSM8K and that the agreement fraction is itself a calibrated confidence; Universal Self-Consistency (Chen et al. 2023) extends this to free-form outputs by letting the LLM pick the most consistent of 8 candidates, and Adaptive-Consistency (Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023) cuts sample budgets up to 7.9x with <0.1% accuracy loss via early stopping. The same disagreement signal, computed over meaning clusters, becomes semantic entropy (Kuhn et al., ICLR 2023; Farquhar et al., Nature 2024, AUROC 0.790 vs 0.69-0.70 for baselines with 10 samples) and SelfCheckGPT (Manakul et al., EMNLP 2023, 93.4 AUC-PR for non-factual sentence detection with 20 samples). (2) Evidence-grounded claim checking: FActScore (Min et al., EMNLP 2023) decomposes text into atomic facts and scores the supported fraction with <2% estimator error; RARR (Gao et al., ACL 2023), FacTool (Chern et al. 2023) and Chain-of-Verification (Dhuliawala et al. 2023) generate verification questions, retrieve or execute tools, and minimally revise (CoVe factor+revise lifts biography FActScore 55.9 -> 71.4; RARR preserves intent >90% while raising attribution ~9-11 points). (3) Learned or prompted verifiers: Cobbe et al. (2021) show a 6B verifier reranking 100 samples beats a 30x larger finetuned model; Lightman et al. (2023) show process supervision (PRM) reaches 78.2% on MATH vs 72.4% ORM and 69.6% majority vote at N=1860; Math-Shepherd, PAVs and Generative Verifiers remove the human-labelling bottleneck and make verifiers scale with test-time compute (Snell et al. 2024: >4x compute-efficiency over best-of-N). LLM-as-a-judge (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2023) achieves 85% agreement with human experts (human-human 81%) but exhibits position bias (GPT-4 only 65% order-consistent), verbosity bias (91% failure on repetition attacks for GPT-3.5/Claude-v1) and self-preference (Panickssery et al. 2024). (4) Calibration: RLHF models verbalize better-calibrated confidence than their token probabilities (Tian et al., EMNLP 2023, ~50% relative ECE reduction with top-k verbalization) but remain overconfident (Xiong et al., ICLR 2024), so confidence should be fused from consistency plus verbalized estimates and used for routing, not trusted alone.

The most important negative result for production design is that intrinsic self-correction does not work: Huang et al. (ICLR 2024) show GPT-4 drops from 95.5% to 89.0% on GSM8K after two rounds of unassisted self-correction and that multi-agent debate underperforms plain self-consistency at equal sample budgets (83.0% vs 88.2% at 9 responses); Self-Refine's math gain was 0-0.2 points because the model declared "everything looks good" 94% of the time; CRITIC's ablation shows self-critique without tools gives negligible or negative gains; Olausson et al. (ICLR 2024) show code self-repair is "often modest" and bottlenecked by feedback quality; Kamoi et al. (TACL 2024) find no successful prompted self-correction outside tasks with reliable external feedback. The state of the art is therefore a verification component built around external or statistical evidence (execution, retrieval, agreement, trained verifiers), with LLM judgment used only in debiased, rubric-anchored, reference-guided form, with explicit confidence gating and abstention, and with the budget allocated adaptively per query.

1. **Xuezhi Wang, Jason Wei, Dale Schuurmans, Quoc Le, Ed Chi, Sharan Narang, Aakanksha Chowdhery, Denny Zhou (2022).** *Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models.* ICLR 2023. arXiv:2203.11171  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: 7,953 citations (Semantic Scholar, Oct 2026); ICLR 2023; the canonical sampling-based verification baseline
   - Key contribution: Replaces greedy CoT decoding with sampling 40 diverse reasoning paths (T=0.7, top-k 40 for PaLM-540B; T=0.7 for GPT-3) and majority-voting the final answer. Gains over CoT: GSM8K +17.9 (PaLM-540B 56.5 -> 74.4; code-davinci-002 60.1 -> 78.0), SVAMP +11.0, AQuA +12.2, StrategyQA +6.4, ARC-c +3.9. Accuracy saturates quickly (5-10 paths capture most of the gain) and the fraction of samples agreeing with the majority is highly correlated with accuracy, so it doubles as a confidence score.
   - Design implication: Make k-sample majority vote the default verification primitive for any task with an extractable, normalizable answer (numbers, labels, SQL results, test outcomes). Use the agreement ratio as a free confidence signal to drive routing and abstention. Start at k=5-10 and only scale to 20-40 for hard, high-stakes queries.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2203.11171: title match 1.00, year 2022

2. **Xinyun Chen, Renat Aksitov, Uri Alon, Jie Ren, Kefan Xiao, Pengcheng Yin, Sushant Prakash, Charles Sutton, Xuezhi Wang, Denny Zhou (2023).** *Universal Self-Consistency for Large Language Model Generation.* arXiv preprint (Google DeepMind). arXiv:2311.17311  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17311>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Widely cited follow-up to Self-Consistency from the same group; adopted in practice for free-form voting
   - Key contribution: Lets the LLM select the most consistent answer among 8 sampled candidates instead of requiring an extractable answer. Matches SC on math (PaLM 2-L GSM8K: SC 90.4 vs USC 90.2; MATH 37.9 vs 37.4) and matches execution-based voting on code without running code (BIRD-SQL 45.6 vs 45.5; ARCADE 30.3 vs 30.1), and improves free-form tasks where SC does not apply (GovReport ROUGE-1 38.8 -> 40.2; TruthfulQA GPT-judge 62.1 -> 67.7 for PaLM 2-L). Limitations: candidates are bounded by context length, no confidence estimate is produced, gains plateau after ~5 samples on summarization and GSM8K dropped at 16 samples.
   - Design implication: Use USC as the aggregation fallback when answers cannot be canonicalized (summaries, specs, incident narratives, design rationales). Cap candidates at 5-8, randomize their order, and pair it with a separate confidence estimator since USC yields none.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2311.17311: title match 1.00, year 2023

3. **Pranjal Aggarwal, Aman Madaan, Yiming Yang, Mausam (2023).** *Let's Sample Step by Step: Adaptive-Consistency for Efficient Reasoning and Coding with LLMs.* EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2305.11860  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11860>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: EMNLP 2023 main; standard reference for cost-aware self-consistency
   - Key contribution: Allocates samples per question with a lightweight stopping criterion based on the agreement observed so far (posterior probability that the current majority is stable). Across 17 reasoning and code datasets and three LLMs, it reduces the sample budget by up to 7.9x with an average accuracy drop below 0.1%.
   - Design implication: Never use a fixed k in production: sample sequentially (or in small parallel batches) and stop when the majority's posterior stability exceeds a threshold (~0.95). This turns verification cost from O(k_max) per query into a budget concentrated on genuinely ambiguous queries.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2305.11860: title match 1.00, year 2023

4. **Shehzaad Dhuliawala, Mojtaba Komeili, Jing Xu, Roberta Raileanu, Xian Li, Asli Celikyilmaz, Jason Weston (2023).** *Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models.* arXiv preprint (Meta AI); Findings of ACL 2024. arXiv:2309.11495  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Hundreds of citations; widely adopted verification-prompting pattern
   - Key contribution: Four steps: draft baseline response; plan verification questions; execute verifications (joint, 2-step, factored, factor+revise variants); generate final verified response. With Llama 65B: Wikidata list precision 0.17 -> 0.36 (2-step); Wiki-Category 0.12 -> 0.22 (factored); MultiSpanQA F1 0.39 -> 0.48 (factored); biography FActScore 55.9 -> 63.7 (factored) -> 71.4 (factor+revise). Factored variants beat joint because answering verification questions without the draft in context prevents the model from copying its own hallucinations. Average fact count fell 16.6 -> 12.3 (precision gained partly by dropping claims).
   - Design implication: Implement verification questions as isolated calls that do not see the original draft (factored), then an explicit cross-check step (factor+revise). Track recall/claim-count alongside precision so the component is not rewarded for simply deleting content.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2309.11495: title match 1.00, year 2023

5. **Ruochen Zhao, Xingxuan Li, Shafiq Joty, Chengwei Qin, Lidong Bing (2023).** *Verify-and-Edit: A Knowledge-Enhanced Chain-of-Thought Framework.* arXiv preprint (ACL 2023). arXiv:2305.03268  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03268>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Several hundred citations; one of the first retrieval-grounded CoT post-editing frameworks
   - Key contribution: Post-edits CoT rationales with external knowledge: self-consistency agreement is used to decide which questions are uncertain (low agreement), then verification questions are generated, retrieved against (search/Wikipedia), and the rationale is edited before re-deriving the answer. Reports accuracy improvements on multiple open-domain QA tasks with GPT-3.
   - Design implication: Use agreement as a trigger: only run expensive retrieval-grounded editing on low-consistency outputs. This is the canonical 'uncertainty-gated verification' pattern and directly ties the verification component to hybrid retrieval.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2305.03268: title match 1.00, year 2023

6. **Ofir Press, Muru Zhang, Sewon Min, Ludwig Schmidt, Noah A. Smith, Mike Lewis (2022).** *Measuring and Narrowing the Compositionality Gap in Language Models (Self-Ask).* Findings of EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2210.03350  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03350>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Widely cited; Self-Ask is a standard decomposition-plus-check prompt pattern
   - Key contribution: Defines the compositionality gap (sub-questions right, composition wrong), shows it does not shrink with scale across GPT-3 sizes, and introduces self-ask: the model explicitly poses and answers follow-up questions before the final answer, which beats chain-of-thought; the structured follow-ups can be routed to a search engine, further improving accuracy.
   - Design implication: Structure verification as explicit sub-questions that can be dispatched to tools/retrieval rather than one monolithic 'is this correct?' prompt. The explicit structure also makes verification traces auditable.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2210.03350: title match 0.94, year 2022

7. **Potsawee Manakul, Adian Liusie, Mark J. F. Gales (2023).** *SelfCheckGPT: Zero-Resource Black-Box Hallucination Detection for Generative Large Language Models.* EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2303.08896  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08896>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: EMNLP 2023 main; >1,000 citations; standard black-box hallucination baseline
   - Key contribution: Samples N=20 stochastic responses (T=1.0) and checks each sentence of the main (T=0, beam) response against them with BERTScore, QA, n-gram, NLI, or an LLM prompt. On WikiBio GPT-3 passages: non-factual sentence AUC-PR 93.42 (Prompt), 92.50 (NLI) vs 87.51 for the best grey-box token-probability baseline and 72.96 random; passage-level Pearson 78.32 (Prompt) vs 57.83 baseline.
   - Design implication: When token logprobs are unavailable (API models) or untrustworthy, sentence-level consistency against samples is the best zero-resource hallucination detector. The NLI variant (DeBERTa) is nearly as good as the LLM-prompt variant at a fraction of the cost and should be the default scorer.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2303.08896: title match 1.00, year 2023

8. **Lorenz Kuhn, Yarin Gal, Sebastian Farquhar (2023).** *Semantic Uncertainty: Linguistic Invariances for Uncertainty Estimation in Natural Language Generation.* ICLR 2023 (Spotlight). arXiv:2302.09664  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09664>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: ICLR 2023 spotlight; >1,000 citations; precursor to the Nature paper
   - Key contribution: Introduces semantic entropy: cluster sampled generations by meaning (bidirectional entailment) and compute entropy over clusters rather than surface strings. Unsupervised, single-model, no modification to off-the-shelf LLMs; more predictive of accuracy than token-level entropy baselines on QA.
   - Design implication: Meaning-level clustering is required for any uncertainty metric over free text; surface-level agreement under-counts agreement among paraphrases and over-estimates uncertainty.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2302.09664: title match 1.00, year 2023

9. **Sebastian Farquhar, Jannik Kossen, Lorenz Kuhn, Yarin Gal (2024).** *Detecting hallucinations in large language models using semantic entropy.* Nature 630, 625-630.  
   <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186750>  _(not independently verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: 1,859 citations (Semantic Scholar, Oct 2026); Nature
   - Key contribution: Targets confabulations (arbitrary, incorrect generations). Uses 10 sampled generations, clusters them by bidirectional entailment (GPT-3.5 or DeBERTa-large-MNLI), and computes entropy over clusters; a discrete variant needs no token probabilities and performs similarly. Averaged over 30 task-model combinations (TriviaQA, SQuAD, BioASQ, NQ-Open, SVAMP; LLaMA 2 Chat, Falcon Instruct, Mistral Instruct), AUROC 0.790 vs naive entropy 0.691, P(True) 0.698, embedding regression 0.687. Extends to paragraphs by decomposing into factoids, generating questions per factoid, and averaging semantic entropy; beats self-check and P(True) baselines on AUROC and AURAC.
   - Design implication: Implement discrete semantic entropy as the standard uncertainty score for black-box models: N=10 samples, NLI-based bidirectional entailment clustering, entropy over cluster counts. For long outputs, apply per atomic claim and aggregate; use the score to trigger retrieval verification or abstention.
   - Verification evidence: no arXiv identifier; needs venue-page check

10. **Sewon Min, Kalpesh Krishna, Xinxi Lyu, Mike Lewis, Wen-tau Yih, Pang Wei Koh, Mohit Iyyer, Luke Zettlemoyer, Hannaneh Hajishirzi (2023).** *FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation.* EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2305.14251  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: 1,823 citations (Semantic Scholar, Oct 2026); EMNLP 2023 main; pip-installable standard
   - Key contribution: Decomposes a generation into atomic facts, labels each Supported/Not-supported against a knowledge source, and reports the supported fraction over non-abstaining responses. Human FActScore on biographies: InstructGPT ~42.5%, ChatGPT ~58.3%, PerplexityAI ~71.5%; accuracy collapses for rare entities (ChatGPT ~80% -> 16%) and for facts later in the text. Automated estimator (decompose -> retrieve Wikipedia -> LM support judgement, optionally plus nonparametric probability) has <2% error vs humans; evaluating 6,500 generations would have cost ~$26K with humans. FActScore is precision-only, so it must be reported with abstention rate and average fact count.
   - Design implication: Build the attribution scorer as decompose -> retrieve -> entail per atomic claim; always emit three numbers (supported fraction, claim count, abstention) so the pipeline cannot game precision by saying less. Expect lower factuality on long-tail entities (rare part numbers, obscure standards clauses) and route those to retrieval.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2305.14251: title match 1.00, year 2023

11. **Luyu Gao, Zhuyun Dai, Panupong Pasupat, Anthony Chen, Arun Tejasvi Chaganty, Yicheng Fan, Vincent Y. Zhao, Ni Lao, Hongrae Lee, Da-Cheng Juan, Kelvin Guu (2022).** *RARR: Researching and Revising What Language Models Say, Using Language Models.* ACL 2023. arXiv:2210.08726  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08726>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: ACL 2023; several hundred citations; reference architecture for post-hoc attribution and revision
   - Key contribution: Research stage: generate covering questions, web-search each (5 pages/query), keep best snippet. Revision stage: an agreement model flags evidence that contradicts the text; an edit model revises only then, and oversized edits are rejected. Attribution report: up to 5 snippets. Auto-AIS attribution rises NQ 45.6 -> 54.9, SQA 37.6 -> 45.1, QReCC 18.8 -> 29.4 while preserving intent >90% of the time; baselines (EFEC, LaMDA) get higher attribution only by rewriting (preservation 4-34%), giving RARR the best F1_AP (57.0 / 45.9 / 41.5).
   - Design implication: Separate 'agreement check' from 'edit' and only edit on contradiction; enforce an edit-size budget (Levenshtein/preservation) so verification does not silently rewrite the author's intent. Emit the attribution report as a first-class artifact.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2210.08726: title match 1.00, year 2022

12. **I-Chun Chern, Steffi Chern, Shiqi Chen, Weizhe Yuan, Kehua Feng, Chunting Zhou, Junxian He, Graham Neubig, Pengfei Liu (2023).** *FacTool: Factuality Detection in Generative AI -- A Tool Augmented Framework for Multi-Task and Multi-Domain Scenarios.* arXiv preprint. arXiv:2307.13528  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13528>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Several hundred citations; released as open-source library and ChatGPT plugin
   - Key contribution: Five-step, task/domain-agnostic framework: claim extraction, query generation, tool querying, evidence collection, agreement verification. Tools per domain: Google Search (KB-QA), Python interpreter running generated tests with majority-vote pseudo-golden outputs (code), interpreter re-computing each arithmetic step (math), Google Scholar metadata check (literature). Claim/response F1 with GPT-4: KB-QA 89.1/71.8, code 92.1, math 99.0/80.4, sci-lit 95.2/94.7, versus Self-Check (GPT-4) 86.6/57.1, 85.3, 95.8/70.6 and 36.7/21.5 respectively; self-check has lower precision and misses errors.
   - Design implication: This is the template for a pluggable verifier: a claim-type router that dispatches each claim to a domain tool (search, code runner, calculator, catalog/API lookup) then runs a single agreement step. Self-check without tools is the weakest arm and should not be the only verifier for any factual claim.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2307.13528: title match 1.00, year 2023

13. **Tianyu Gao, Howard Yen, Jiatong Yu, Danqi Chen (2023).** *Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations (ALCE).* EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2305.14627  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: EMNLP 2023 main; standard citation-quality benchmark (princeton-nlp/ALCE)
   - Key contribution: First benchmark for automatic evaluation of LLM citations (fluency, correctness, citation recall/precision via NLI), shown to correlate strongly with human judgments. Even the best models lack complete citation support 50% of the time on ELI5; improvements need better retrievers, long-context models and multi-source synthesis.
   - Design implication: Score citation recall and precision separately with an NLI entailment model over (claim, cited passage) pairs; make 'every sentence with a factual claim must be entailed by at least one cited chunk' a hard gate for RAG outputs.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2305.14627: title match 0.96, year 2023

14. **Lianmin Zheng, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ying Sheng, Siyuan Zhuang, Zhanghao Wu, Yonghao Zhuang, Zi Lin, Zhuohan Li, Dacheng Li, Eric P. Xing, Hao Zhang, Joseph E. Gonzalez, Ion Stoica (2023).** *Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena.* NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks. arXiv:2306.05685  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Several thousand citations; the defining LLM-as-a-judge paper; basis of Chatbot Arena
   - Key contribution: GPT-4 pairwise judgments agree with human experts 85% (first and second turn, non-tie), exceeding human-human agreement (81-82%). Documents biases: position bias (order-consistency GPT-4 65.0%, GPT-3.5 46.2%, Claude-v1 23.8%), verbosity bias (repetitive-list attack fooled Claude-v1 and GPT-3.5 91.3% of the time, GPT-4 8.7%), self-enhancement (GPT-4 ~+10%, Claude-v1 ~+25% win rate on own outputs, inconclusive), and limited math/reasoning grading (default prompt failed 14/20 math cases; CoT 6/20; reference-guided 3/20). Mitigations: swap positions and count only consistent wins, few-shot judge (65 -> 77.5% consistency at ~4x cost), CoT, reference-guided grading.
   - Design implication: Any LLM judge in the component must run position-swapped, use reference-guided grading whenever a reference or executed result exists, be a different model family from the generator, and be length-controlled. Treat the judge as a noisy ~85%-agreement sensor, not ground truth.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2306.05685: title match 1.00, year 2023

15. **Peiyi Wang, Lei Li, Liang Chen, Zefan Cai, Dawei Zhu, Binghuai Lin, Yunbo Cao, Qi Liu, Tianyu Liu, Zhifang Sui (2023).** *Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators.* arXiv preprint (ACL 2024). arXiv:2305.17926  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Several hundred citations; the standard reference for position-bias calibration
   - Key contribution: Shows rankings can be flipped by candidate order: Vicuna-13B 'beat' ChatGPT on 66/80 queries when order favored it. Proposes Multiple Evidence Calibration (generate evidence before scoring), Balanced Position Calibration (aggregate across both orders) and Human-in-the-Loop Calibration using a balanced position diversity entropy to flag hard cases for humans.
   - Design implication: Use order-swap aggregation by default and compute the position-diversity entropy as an escalation signal: items whose verdict depends on order go to a human or a stronger verifier.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2305.17926: title match 1.00, year 2023

16. **Arjun Panickssery, Samuel R. Bowman, Shi Feng (2024).** *LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations.* arXiv preprint (NeurIPS 2024). arXiv:2404.13076  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076>  _(verified)_
   - Why it is highly rated: Widely cited bias study; informs judge-selection practice
   - Key contribution: GPT-4 and Llama 2 distinguish their own outputs from others with non-trivial accuracy out of the box; fine-tuning reveals a linear relationship between self-recognition ability and self-preference bias, with controlled experiments indicating the link is causal rather than confounded.
   - Design implication: Never let the same model (or family) that generated a candidate be the sole judge of it; use cross-family judges or non-LLM verifiers (execution, retrieval entailment) for the final gate.

17. **Yann Dubois, Balázs Galambosi, Percy Liang, Tatsunori B. Hashimoto (2024).** *Length-Controlled AlpacaEval: A Simple Way to Debias Automatic Evaluators.* COLM 2024. arXiv:2404.04475

   - Why it is highly rated: COLM 2024; adopted as the default AlpacaEval leaderboard metric
   - Key contribution: Fits a GLM predicting judge preference from length difference and other features, then reports the counterfactual preference at zero length difference. Makes the metric robust to verbosity manipulation and raises Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena from 0.94 to 0.98.
   - Design implication: Log output lengths and apply a length-controlled correction (or explicit length-normalized rubric) to any pairwise judge score used for selection among candidates.
   - Verification evidence: arXiv 2404.04475: title match 1.00, year 2024

18. **Yang Liu, Dan Iter, Yichong Xu, Shuohang Wang, Ruochen Xu, Chenguang Zhu (2023).** *G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment.* EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2303.16634

   - Why it is highly rated: EMNLP 2023; >1,000 citations; widely implemented in eval libraries
   - Key contribution: Rubric-based LLM evaluation: auto-generated CoT evaluation steps plus a form-filling score, with final score computed as the probability-weighted sum over score tokens to reduce ties. 21. **Zhibin Gou, Zhihong Shao, Yeyun Gong, Yelong Shen, Yujiu Yang, Nan Duan, Weizhu Chen (2023).** *CRITIC: Large Language Models Can Self-Correct with Tool-Interactive Critiquing.* ICLR 2024. arXiv:2305.11738  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738>  _(verified)_

## Implementable techniques

- **Adaptive Self-Consistency (sample-vote with early stopping)** - 1) Canonicalize the task so a final answer is extractable (number, label, SQL result set, test verdict, normalized JSON). 2) Sample reasoning paths at T~0.7 (top-k 40 or top-p 0.95), in batches of b (e.g., 3), using diverse prompt variants to decorrelate samples. 3) After each batch, canonicalize answers and compute the posterior probability that the current majority answer is stable (Adaptive-Consistency: Dirichlet/beta posterior over answer counts; stop when P(majority stays top) >= threshold). 4) Stop at threshold or k_max; return majority answer and confidence = agreement fraction (and the posterior). 5) If agreement < c_low, flag as uncertain and route to heavier verification (retrieval/tool) or abstain.
  - When to use: Any task with a canonicalizable answer: numeric/engineering calculations, classification (severity, root-cause category), extraction fields, SQL/code with executable result, multiple-choice compliance checks.
  - Trade-offs: Cost scales with k (k LLM calls); gains saturate quickly (5-10 paths capture most). Fails when errors are systematic (all samples share the same misconception) and when answers cannot be normalized. Provides the cheapest reliable confidence signal; at equal budget it beats multi-agent debate (Huang et al.).
  - Literature defaults: k_min=3, k_max=10 (up to 40 for high-stakes), batch=3, temperature 0.7, stopping threshold 0.95 posterior (Adaptive-Consistency reports up to 7.9x fewer samples with <0.1% accuracy loss), low-agreement trigger c_low=0.6. Self-Consistency used 40 paths and found saturation by 5-10.
  - Sources: Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models; Let's Sample Step by Step: Adaptive-Consistency for Efficient Reasoning and Coding with LLMs; Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet

- **Universal Self-Consistency / LLM-selected consensus for free-form outputs** - 1) Generate n candidates (T~0.7). 2) Build a single selection prompt listing candidates in randomized order with index labels, asking the model to identify the response most consistent with the majority and output only its index (optionally with a short rationale). 3) Run the selection 2-3 times with shuffled orders; take the modal selection. 4) Return the selected candidate; because USC yields no confidence, attach confidence from a separate estimator (semantic entropy over the candidates or verbalized confidence).
  - When to use: Summaries, specifications, incident narratives, design rationales, code where execution is unavailable; whenever majority vote cannot be computed.
  - Trade-offs: One extra long-context call; candidates limited by context window; consistency != quality (oracle 96.2 vs USC 90.2 on GSM8K); selection has position bias so order must be randomized; gains plateau around 5 candidates and can drop at 16.
  - Literature defaults: n=5-8 candidates (paper default 8; summarization gains flat after 5), 2-3 shuffled selection passes, selection at temperature 0, cap total candidate tokens at ~50% of context window.
  - Sources: Universal Self-Consistency for Large Language Model Generation; Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena

- **Discrete Semantic Entropy / SelfCheckGPT hallucination scoring** - 1) Produce the primary answer (T=0 or low). 2) Sample N stochastic answers (T=1.0). 3) For short answers: cluster the N samples by bidirectional entailment using an NLI model (DeBERTa-large-MNLI) or an LLM judge; compute discrete semantic entropy H = -sum_c (n_c/N) log(n_c/N) over clusters. 4) For long answers: split the primary answer into sentences or atomic claims; for each, compute SelfCheck-NLI score = mean over samples of P(contradiction | sample, claim) (or generate a question per claim, sample answers, and compute semantic entropy per claim); aggregate per passage by mean and max. 5) Threshold: claims above tau are marked unsupported and routed to retrieval verification or flagged; passage score drives abstention.
  - When to use: Black-box or API models without trustworthy logprobs; open-domain or long-form generation where no reference exists; triage before expensive retrieval checks; detecting confabulation on long-tail entities (part numbers, obscure clauses).
  - Trade-offs: N extra generations per query plus O(N^2) NLI pairs for clustering (cheap with a small NLI model). Detects arbitrary confabulations, not consistent misconceptions. NLI variant is nearly as strong as LLM-prompt variant at far lower cost (92.5 vs 93.4 AUC-PR).
  - Literature defaults: N=10 samples for semantic entropy (Nature paper), N=20 for SelfCheckGPT; sampling T=1.0; NLI = DeBERTa-large-MNLI with bidirectional entailment required for same-cluster; threshold tau tuned on a labelled dev set to the desired recall of hallucinated claims (target AUROC ~0.79 reported on sentence-length tasks); passage aggregation = mean of sentence scores.
  - Sources: Detecting hallucinations in large language models using semantic entropy; Semantic Uncertainty: Linguistic Invariances for Uncertainty Estimation in Natural Language Generation; SelfCheckGPT: Zero-Resource Black-Box Hallucination Detection for Generative Large Language Models

- **Factored Chain-of-Verification with minimal revision** - 1) Draft response. 2) Plan: generate verification questions covering each factual claim (one per atomic claim). 3) Execute factored: answer each question in an isolated call that does NOT include the draft (prevents copying hallucinations); optionally route each question to retrieval or a tool instead of the model's memory (Self-Ask style). 4) Cross-check (factor+revise): for each (claim, verified answer) pair, an explicit consistency call labels agree/contradict/unknown. 5) Revise: edit only contradicted or unsupported claims with the smallest change that restores support (RARR edit budget); drop or hedge if no support is found. 6) Report claim count before/after so recall loss is visible.
  - When to use: Long-form factual outputs: specification summaries, standards interpretations, incident reports, BOM descriptions, status write-ups; especially when uncertainty signals (low agreement, high semantic entropy) are raised.
  - Trade-offs: Cost grows with the number of claims (one or more calls each); may reduce recall (CoVe fact count 16.6 -> 12.3); without tools it only removes what the model can recognize as wrong. Factored variants cost more than joint but are markedly more accurate.
  - Literature defaults: One verification question per atomic claim, max 10-15 questions per response (batch beyond that); verification calls at T=0 without the draft in context; use 2-step/factored not joint; edit acceptance requires preservation >= 0.8 on a Levenshtein-ratio (RARR rejects oversized edits); trigger only when agreement < 0.6 or semantic entropy above tau (Verify-and-Edit).
  - Sources: Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models; RARR: Researching and Revising What Language Models Say, Using Language Models; Measuring and Narrowing the Compositionality Gap in Language Models (Self-Ask); Verify-and-Edit: A Knowledge-Enhanced Chain-of-Thought Framework

- **Atomic-claim attribution scoring with tool routing (FActScore/FacTool/ALCE)** - 1) Decompose output into atomic claims (LLM, few-shot). 2) Classify each claim by type (textual fact, numeric/arithmetic, code behavior, citation/reference, standard clause, catalog attribute). 3) Route: textual -> hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense) over the corpus, top-5 chunks; numeric -> calculator/Python recompute; code -> execute generated tests; citation -> metadata lookup (doc registry, Scholar, PLM/ERP API); clause -> fetch the exact clause text. 4) Entailment: an NLI model or LLM labels Supported/Contradicted/No-evidence per claim given the evidence. 5) Score: attribution precision = supported/total, citation recall = fraction of claims with at least one entailing cited chunk, citation precision = fraction of citations that entail something; report with claim count and abstention rate. 6) Gate: block or revise if precision < p_min or any Contradicted claims exist.
  - When to use: All RAG outputs and any output making checkable factual, numeric, or code claims; compliance-sensitive engineering documents.
  - Trade-offs: Most expensive family (decomposition + retrieval + entailment per claim) but highest precision; quality bounded by retriever coverage (claims about undocumented facts become No-evidence, not Contradicted); FActScore is precision-only and can be gamed by brevity.
  - Literature defaults: Top-5 evidence passages per claim (RARR used 5 pages/query, up to 5 snippets in the report); NLI threshold for 'Supported' at P(entail) >= 0.5 with bidirectional check for equivalence claims; gate p_min = 0.9 for engineering documentation, 1.0 (no Contradicted) for compliance; FActScore estimator error <2% vs humans; always report abstention % and mean claims per response.
  - Sources: FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation; FacTool: Factuality Detection in Generative AI -- A Tool Augmented Framework for Multi-Task and Multi-Domain Scenarios; Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations (ALCE); Reduce hallucinations (Claude Developer Platform documentation)

- **Debiased, reference-guided LLM-as-judge (pairwise and rubric)** - Pairwise: 1) Present candidates A/B with a task-specific rubric; 2) ask for step-by-step evaluation then a verdict; 3) run again with A/B swapped; 4) only count a win if both orders agree, else tie; 5) use a judge from a different model family than the generator; 6) apply a length-controlled correction (regress out length difference) or instruct explicitly to ignore length and penalize padding. Rubric scoring (G-Eval): 1) define criteria and auto-generated evaluation steps; 2) form-fill a 1-5 score; 3) compute expected score from token probabilities or average over 5-10 samples; 4) when a reference exists (executed result, gold spec, oracle value), use reference-guided grading. Judge calibration: maintain a human-labelled anchor set and track agreement; escalate items with high position-diversity entropy to humans.
  - When to use: Selecting among free-form candidates, quality gating of documents, scoring against domain rubrics (clarity, completeness, standards compliance), and as the cheap pre-filter before expensive tool checks.
  - Trade-offs: 2x calls for swap, ~4x for few-shot judging; ~85% agreement with experts at best; biased on math/reasoning without references (70% failure default, 15% reference-guided); prefers LLM-style and verbose text; self-preference if same family.
  - Literature defaults: Always 2 orderings; few-shot judge only for high-value items (+12.5 points consistency at 4x cost); rubric score as probability-weighted expectation over 1-5; 5 judge samples at T=0.7 averaged if logprobs unavailable; length-control via GLM fit on >=200 pairs; cross-family judge mandatory; human anchor set >= 100 items refreshed quarterly.
  - Sources: Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena; Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators; G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment; Length-Controlled AlpacaEval: A Simple Way to Debias Automatic Evaluators; LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations; Prometheus: Inducing Fine-grained Evaluation Capability in Language Models

- **Verifier-guided Best-of-N with process reward models and weighted voting** - 1) Generate N candidate solutions with step-delimited reasoning. 2) Score each step with a PRM (trained on human step labels like PRM800K, or automatic labels via Math-Shepherd-style Monte Carlo rollouts, or progress/advantage labels via PAVs); aggregate per solution with min or product of step scores. 3) Weighted vote: group candidates by canonical final answer and sum PRM scores per group (DiVeRSe/Cobbe top-k voting); return the top group's best-scoring solution. 4) Use the PRM score distribution to estimate difficulty and adapt N (Snell et al.): small N for confidently-high scores, larger N or beam search for mid-range, escalate when all scores are low. 5) Optionally use a generative verifier that emits a rationale and P(correct), and sample it k times for hard items.
  - When to use: High-volume, well-defined reasoning domains with a correctness oracle for training data (calculations, structured analysis, code with tests, requirement-to-test tracing) where a dedicated verifier can be trained or an open PRM reused.
  - Trade-offs: Requires training data and MLOps for the verifier; N generations per query; verifier performance can degrade beyond a few hundred samples (Cobbe); PRM generalization is better than ORM but still domain-sensitive; large gains (PRM 78.2 vs majority 69.6 at N=1860; GenRM GSM8K 73 -> 93.4).
  - Literature defaults: N=16-64 in production (paper regimes 100-1860); aggregation = min of step scores (77.6%) or product (78.2%); weighted vote over top 3-5 verifier-ranked candidates (Cobbe); difficulty buckets by max PRM score (e.g., >0.9 easy: N=4; 0.5-0.9: N=16-32; <0.5: escalate); active learning on confident-wrong samples (~2.6x label efficiency).
  - Sources: Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems; Let's Verify Step by Step; Making Large Language Models Better Reasoners with Step-Aware Verifier (DiVeRSe); Math-Shepherd: Verify and Reinforce LLMs Step-by-step without Human Annotations; Rewarding Progress: Scaling Automated Process Verifiers for LLM Reasoning; Generative Verifiers: Reward Modeling as Next-Token Prediction; Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters

- **Tool-grounded verify-and-repair loop (execution, tests, retrieval)** - 1) Generate candidate artifact(s) and, separately, verification tests/queries (CodeT: generate test cases; FacTool: generate queries). 2) Execute: run tests in a sandbox, re-compute numbers, query retrieval/APIs. 3) Score by dual execution agreement: candidates that pass the same generated tests and produce identical outputs cluster together; rank clusters by (tests passed x cluster size). 4) If the best cluster fails required tests, repair with the concrete failure signal (stack trace, failing assertion, contradicting passage), preferring a stronger critic model for feedback; repair at most R rounds. 5) Localize before repairing: pass the failing test/step location explicitly (Tyen et al.). 6) Stop when tests pass or R reached; if still failing, abstain/escalate.
  - When to use: Code, SQL, configuration, CAD/parametric scripts, calculations, anything with an executable or queryable oracle; also factual QA with a retrievable corpus.
  - Trade-offs: Needs sandboxing and tool infrastructure; generated tests can be wrong (mitigated by dual agreement); repair rounds have diminishing returns (0.97-1.05x baseline at equal budget for GPT-4 on APPS); without tools the loop regresses (CRITIC ablation).
  - Literature defaults: Candidates n=10, generated tests t=10-20 per task, repair rounds R<=2 (Olausson: breadth beats depth), repair only on external failure signals, feedback from a stronger or different model when available (repair success 4.7% -> 11.5% with GPT-4 feedback), sandbox timeout per execution 10-30 s.
  - Sources: CodeT: Code Generation with Generated Tests; CRITIC: Large Language Models Can Self-Correct with Tool-Interactive Critiquing; Is Self-Repair a Silver Bullet for Code Generation?; LLMs cannot find reasoning errors, but can correct them given the error location; When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes? A Critical Survey of Self-Correction of LLMs

- **Confidence elicitation, fusion and abstention gating** - 1) Verbalized confidence: in one call, ask for top-k candidate answers each with a numeric probability (k=4); take the top candidate's probability. 2) Consistency confidence: agreement fraction from self-consistency or 1 - normalized semantic entropy. 3) P(True): show the model several of its own samples and ask P(True) for the chosen one (Kadavath). 4) Fuse by logistic regression or isotonic regression trained on a labelled dev set (features: verbalized p, agreement, entropy, verifier score, retrieval support fraction); apply temperature scaling. 5) Gate: release if fused p >= p_release; run heavier verification if p_mid <= p < p_release; abstain/escalate to human if p < p_mid. 6) Monitor ECE, Brier and AUROC of the fused score weekly and recalibrate.
  - When to use: Every verified output should carry a calibrated confidence for routing, SLA-based escalation and audit; essential when the pipeline must decide between answering, retrieving more, or deferring to an engineer.
  - Trade-offs: Verbalized confidence alone is overconfident and weak on professional-knowledge tasks; consistency needs extra samples; calibration drifts across model versions and domains and must be re-fit; fused scores are only as good as the labelled calibration set.
  - Literature defaults: Verbalized 1-stage top-k with k=4 and numeric probabilities (Tian: ECE ~0.04-0.06 on TriviaQA/SciQ for GPT-4 vs 0.08-0.22 for label probabilities); temperature scaling on >=500 labelled items; default thresholds p_release=0.85, p_mid=0.5 (tune to cost of error); target ECE <= 0.05 and AUROC >= 0.8 for failure prediction.
  - Sources: Just Ask for Calibration: Strategies for Eliciting Calibrated Confidence Scores from Language Models Fine-Tuned with Human Feedback; Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty? An Empirical Evaluation of Confidence Elicitation in LLMs; Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know; Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models

- **Multi-agent debate with summarization and adaptive termination** - 1) Instantiate A agents (ideally different models or prompts) that each answer independently. 2) Each round, give every agent a summary (not concatenation) of the other agents' current answers and ask it to update or defend with reasons. 3) Terminate when answers converge (all agree) or after R rounds. 4) A judge from a different model family (or majority vote if canonicalizable) produces the final answer. 5) Always benchmark against adaptive self-consistency at matched call count before enabling in production.
  - When to use: Open-ended judgment tasks (design trade-off reviews, root-cause hypotheses, risk assessments) where diversity of perspective matters and no oracle exists; red-team style review of a plan.
  - Trade-offs: Cost = A x R calls plus summaries; at equal budget underperforms self-consistency on canonicalizable tasks (83.0 vs 88.2 on GSM8K at 9 responses); degeneration-of-thought if agents converge too early; unfair judging if judge shares a model with one debater.
  - Literature defaults: A=3 agents, R=2 rounds (Du et al. default), max R=4 (plateau), summarize peer answers when A>=3, adaptive stop on unanimous agreement, cross-family judge, moderate 'tit-for-tat' disagreement prompt.
  - Sources: Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate; Encouraging Divergent Thinking in Large Language Models through Multi-Agent Debate; Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet

- **Sentence-granular NLI faithfulness scoring (SummaC/TRUE-style grounding check)** - 1) Split the candidate into sentences or atomic claims c_i and the evidence (retrieved chunks, tool output) into sentences e_j. 2) Run an NLI model (TRUE-trained T5-11B or DeBERTa-v3-large MNLI/ANLI) on every (e_j premise, c_i hypothesis) pair, obtaining P(entail), P(contradict). 3) Aggregate per claim: support_i = max_j P(entail_ij); contradiction_i = max_j P(contradict_ij). 4) Passage score = mean_i support_i (SummaC-ZS) or a small learned convolution over the per-claim histogram (SummaC-Conv). 5) Claims with support_i < t_s and contradiction_i < t_c are No-evidence; contradiction_i >= t_c are Contradicted. 6) Only No-evidence/Contradicted claims proceed to LLM-based verification or revision.
  - When to use: Every RAG or document-grounded output, as the cheap first-pass grounding gate before any LLM call; summarization, spec extraction, log-grounded incident narratives.
  - Trade-offs: O(|claims| x |evidence sentences|) small-model inferences (milliseconds each on GPU); misses cross-sentence reasoning and numeric/unit equivalences; NLI models trained on short premises degrade on long or tabular evidence (chunk evidence to <= 300 tokens). Far cheaper and more reproducible than LLM judging; TRUE shows large NLI is competitive with QA-based metrics.
  - Literature defaults: Evidence chunk <= 300 tokens; max-aggregation over evidence sentences (SummaC); Supported if max P(entail) >= 0.5, Contradicted if max P(contradict) >= 0.5 (tune on TRUE-style dev set to ~0.8 ROC AUC); use TRUE's T5-11B ANLI model or DeBERTa-v3-large-MNLI; ensemble NLI + QG/QA score for the final gate (TRUE finds them complementary).
  - Sources: SummaC: Re-Visiting NLI-based Models for Inconsistency Detection in Summarization; TRUE: Re-evaluating Factual Consistency Evaluation; Measuring Attribution in Natural Language Generation Models (AIS)

- **Verifier over-optimization (Goodhart) guard for best-of-N and repair loops** - 1) Treat every learned verifier (ORM/PRM/LLM judge) as a proxy reward r_proxy. 2) Hold a small 'gold' channel: execution/tests, retrieval entailment, or periodic human labels on a random sample of selected outputs. 3) For each verifier version, sweep N on a dev set and record gold accuracy vs N; fit the Gao et al. BoN form and locate N* where gold accuracy peaks. 4) Set production N_max <= N* (Cobbe observed decline past ~400 for a 6B verifier; Stroebl et al. find optimal N often < 10 for weak test suites). 5) In production, monitor proxy score of selected outputs vs gold outcome; alert when the proxy-gold gap drifts. 6) Never run RL or iterative optimization of the generator directly against the learned verifier; optimize only against rule-based/deterministic rewards (DeepSeek-R1 practice).
  - When to use: Whenever a learned verifier or LLM judge is used to select among many candidates, rank repairs, or provide training signal; mandatory before increasing N beyond ~16.
  - Trade-offs: Requires a gold channel (cost of tests/human labels); N* depends on verifier size and domain so must be re-swept per verifier version. Prevents the silent accuracy regressions that larger N otherwise causes; imperfect-verifier false positives also carry style/convention debt.
  - Literature defaults: Sweep N in {1,4,16,64,256} per verifier release; N_max = argmax gold accuracy (typically 16-64 for 1B-7B verifiers; <= 10 when the verifier is a generated test suite); gold audit sample >= 200 items/week; alert if proxy-gold correlation drops > 0.1 from baseline; forbid RL against learned verifiers, allow only rule-based rewards.
  - Sources: Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization; The Limits of Inference Scaling Through Resampling (Inference Scaling fLaws); Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems; DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

- **Panel of diverse LLM judges (PoLL) with disagreement escalation** - 1) Select 3 (or 5) judge models from disjoint model families, none sharing a family with the generator. 2) Give each judge the same debiased prompt (rubric, reference if available, position-swapped pairwise). 3) Aggregate: majority vote for pairwise/binary verdicts, mean of scores for Likert, after per-judge z-normalization. 4) Compute panel disagreement (entropy of votes or score variance). 5) Route high-disagreement items to a stronger judge or human; low-disagreement items are final. 6) Track per-judge agreement with the human anchor set and drop/replace drifting judges.
  - When to use: All LLM-judge scoring in the pipeline where no deterministic oracle exists; especially when the generator is a frontier model whose family would otherwise be the only capable judge.
  - Trade-offs: 3-5 smaller-model calls instead of one large call (Verga et al. report >7x lower cost than GPT-4 alone with better human correlation); more orchestration and more prompts to maintain; panel members may share training data and still correlate. Structurally removes single-judge self-preference.
  - Literature defaults: 3 judges from 3 families (e.g., one each from Anthropic/OpenAI/Google or open models), each run in both orders; majority of 6 votes; escalate if vote split is 3-3 or score std > 1 on a 1-5 scale; refresh judge roster and anchor-set agreement quarterly.
  - Sources: Replacing Judges with Juries: Evaluating LLM Generations with a Panel of Diverse Models; LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations; Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena

- **Conformal factuality back-off (statistically guaranteed abstention)** - 1) Decompose output into sub-claims and score each with the fused confidence s(c) (NLI support, verifier score, semantic entropy, etc.). 2) On a labelled calibration set of n outputs (claims labelled correct/incorrect), compute for each output the minimum threshold tau_i such that all retained claims (s >= tau_i) are correct. 3) Choose tau_hat as the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))/n empirical quantile of {tau_i}. 4) At inference, drop (or hedge) every sub-claim with s(c) < tau_hat; the retained output is then correct with probability >= 1-alpha (exchangeability assumption). 5) Recalibrate when the model, retriever or domain changes; monitor empirical error on audited samples.
  - When to use: Compliance, standards and safety-relevant documents where a stated error-rate guarantee is required; as the principled replacement for ad hoc p_release thresholds.
  - Trade-offs: Guarantee is marginal (averaged over inputs) and assumes calibration data is exchangeable with production traffic; aggressive alpha removes much content (Mohri & Hashimoto retain most content at 80-90% guarantees on FActScore/NQ/MATH); needs claim-level labels (~ hundreds of outputs). Converts verification into an auditable statistical contract.
  - Literature defaults: alpha = 0.1-0.2 for engineering docs, 0.05 for compliance assertions; calibration set >= 300 outputs with claim labels; recalibrate per model/retriever version and monthly; report retained-claim fraction alongside the guarantee so brevity gaming is visible.
  - Sources: Language Models with Conformal Factuality Guarantees; FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation

- **Rule-based verifiable-reward checking (deterministic verifiers with canonicalization)** - 1) For each output type define a deterministic checker: numeric (parse, unit-normalize, compare within tolerance), symbolic (canonicalize expressions before equality), code (compile, run hidden plus generated tests in sandbox), structured (JSON schema, SQL result-set equality), format (required sections/tags present). 2) Require the generator to emit answers in a parseable envelope (boxed answer, fenced code, JSON) and give a format verdict separately from a correctness verdict. 3) Run checkers before any learned verifier; a deterministic Fail is terminal, a Pass is necessary but not sufficient. 4) Log checker coverage (fraction of claims/outputs that have a deterministic checker) as a metric and push coverage up over time. 5) Only deterministic checkers may be used as rewards in any optimization loop.
  - When to use: Calculations, code, SQL, configuration, schema-constrained extraction; any domain where part of the output can be checked mechanically.
  - Trade-offs: Checkers encode domain knowledge and need maintenance; false negatives from brittle parsing (mitigate with canonicalization libraries and tolerance); hidden-test coverage bounds false positives (Stroebl et al.). Zero LLM cost, no bias, immune to persuasion; the only verifier class safe to scale sampling against.
  - Literature defaults: Numeric tolerance 1e-6 relative or domain-specified; unit normalization via a units library; sandbox timeout 10-30 s, no network; require >= 1 hidden test plus generated tests with dual-agreement; target deterministic-checker coverage >= 60% of claims in engineering presets; format and correctness verdicts logged separately.
  - Sources: DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning; Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling; CodeT: Code Generation with Generated Tests

- **Step-level verifier training with consensus-filtered labels and error-localization evaluation** - 1) Generate N solutions per problem with the policy. 2) For each step, obtain an MC label (fraction of K rollouts from that step reaching the correct final answer, Math-Shepherd) and an LLM-judge label (is this step correct given prior steps). 3) Keep only steps where both agree (consensus filtering); discard or re-annotate disagreements. 4) Train the PRM on filtered labels to predict earliest-error position (hard label) or step correctness. 5) Evaluate on (a) best-of-N accuracy and (b) earliest-error F1 on a ProcessBench-style held-out set with human-located errors; reject verifiers whose BoN gain comes with poor localization (process-to-outcome drift). 6) Use the verifier's earliest-error output as the location passed to the repair step.
  - When to use: Building or selecting a domain PRM for calculation chains, design-review reasoning, multi-step test-log analysis; whenever the repair step needs an error location.
  - Trade-offs: Consensus filtering discards data (Qwen reports matching judge-label quality with roughly 40% of the data per third-party summaries; verify before relying on the figure); requires an LLM judge pass per step; PRMs generalize poorly outside training distribution (ProcessBench). Produces a verifier that is useful for localization, not just reranking.
  - Literature defaults: K = 8 MC rollouts per step; keep steps where MC says correct (>0) and judge says correct, or MC = 0 and judge marks the same step as first error; evaluate earliest-error F1 on >= 500 human-annotated chains; require localization F1 within 10 points of BoN-selected PRM before deployment; aggregate step scores by min for selection.
  - Sources: The Lessons of Developing Process Reward Models in Mathematical Reasoning; ProcessBench: Identifying Process Errors in Mathematical Reasoning; Math-Shepherd: Verify and Reinforce LLMs Step-by-step without Human Annotations; LLMs cannot find reasoning errors, but can correct them given the error location

- **Reflexion-style episodic memory for evidence-driven repair loops** - 1) After each failed attempt (test failure, contradicted claim, checker error), run a reflection call that converts the concrete feedback into a short structured note: {failure_signal, hypothesized_cause, what_to_change, what_to_keep}. 2) Append the note to an episodic buffer scoped to the task. 3) Condition the next attempt on the task, the last candidate, and the buffer (not on the full prior transcript). 4) Deduplicate reflections that repeat; if two consecutive reflections are near-identical, stop (no new information). 5) Persist buffers keyed by task signature so recurring tasks start with prior lessons. 6) Hard cap on attempts and on buffer length.
  - When to use: Multi-attempt code/config generation with executable tests, agentic tasks with environment feedback, iterative spec drafting against a checker; never without an external failure signal.
  - Trade-offs: One extra short call per attempt; buffers can accumulate wrong hypotheses (mitigate with dedup and caps); gains are bounded by feedback quality (Olausson) and vanish without external feedback (Kamoi). Reduces repeated identical failures and lets a stronger critic's feedback be reused across attempts.
  - Literature defaults: Max 3 attempts (R <= 2 repairs), reflection <= 150 tokens, buffer <= 5 notes, stop when reflection cosine similarity to previous > 0.9 or when the external signal passes; reflections generated at T=0 by a different/stronger model when available.
  - Sources: Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning; Is Self-Repair a Silver Bullet for Code Generation?; When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes? A Critical Survey of Self-Correction of LLMs

- **Adversarial bug-injection evaluation of verifiers (tampering sets)** - 1) Take outputs known to be correct (tests pass, human-verified claims). 2) Have annotators or a 'sneaky' LLM insert subtle, documented defects: off-by-one, wrong unit, swapped sign, misattributed clause number, fabricated citation, plausible-but-wrong constant. 3) Record ground-truth defect location and type. 4) Run the full verifier stack; measure recall per defect type, false-positive rate on untouched outputs, and localization accuracy. 5) Also test persuasion robustness: wrong outputs with confident language, padding, or 'verified' claims inside the text. 6) Gate verifier releases on these metrics and add newly discovered production misses to the set.
  - When to use: Verifier development and regression testing for every preset (code, spec, compliance, BOM); before trusting any judge or critic in a gate.
  - Trade-offs: Annotation effort to craft realistic defects (CriticGPT used contractor tampering; LLM-generated tampering is cheaper but less realistic); injected defects may not match the natural error distribution, so pair with a sample of naturally occurring errors. Gives the only direct measurement of verifier recall, which agreement-with-humans metrics do not provide.
  - Literature defaults: >= 50 tampered items per defect type per preset; target recall >= 0.9 for deterministic-checkable defects and >= 0.7 for semantic defects, FPR <= 0.05 on clean items; include a 'sneaky' set of 100 confident-wrong candidates; rerun on every verifier/model version.
  - Sources: LLM Critics Help Catch LLM Bugs (CriticGPT); Prover-Verifier Games improve legibility of LLM outputs; Evaluating Large Language Models at Evaluating Instruction Following (LLMBar)

- **Single-pass white-box uncertainty probes (semantic entropy probes) as a pre-filter** - 1) Offline: for a self-hosted model, sample 10 generations for a few thousand prompts, compute semantic entropy (bidirectional-entailment clustering), and binarize high/low SE. 2) Extract hidden states (middle-to-late layers, last token of the answer or of the prompt) from a single greedy generation. 3) Train a logistic-regression probe per layer to predict high SE; pick the best layer on held-out data. 4) Online: run the probe on every generation at negligible cost; if predicted SE is high, trigger sampled semantic entropy or retrieval verification; otherwise skip. 5) Monitor probe AUROC against periodic sampled SE to detect drift.
  - When to use: Self-hosted or open-weight models at high QPS where sampling 10 generations per query is too expensive; as the trigger for the dossier's uncertainty-gated verification.
  - Trade-offs: Requires hidden-state access (not available for API models); probes are model-version specific and must be retrained on upgrade; slightly lower AUROC than full SE but generalizes OOD better than accuracy probes. Removes the 5-10x sampling overhead for the majority of easy queries.
  - Literature defaults: Train on 2-5K prompts with 10 samples each; probe = logistic regression on a single layer (select from the last third of layers by held-out AUROC); trigger sampled SE when P(high SE) > 0.5; retrain per model version; validate probe AUROC >= 0.75 against sampled SE before use.
  - Sources: Semantic Entropy Probes: Robust and Cheap Hallucination Detection in LLMs; Detecting hallucinations in large language models using semantic entropy

## Architecture recommendations

- 1. Expose a single Verifier interface: verify(task_prompt, candidates[], context{evidence, tools, rubric, reference?}, policy) -> VerificationReport{selected_candidate, per_claim_verdicts, fused_confidence, cost, trace}. Make the component stateless between calls and pluggable at any pipeline stage (post-retrieval, post-generation, post-decomposition merge).
- 2. Put a planner in front: an LLM 'verification strategist' reads the natural-language task prompt and the candidate(s), classifies the output type (canonicalizable answer, executable artifact, long-form factual, subjective/stylistic, mixed) and the available oracles (tests, retrieval corpus, calculator, reference answer), then emits a verification plan as structured JSON selecting strategies from a registry (adaptive-SC, USC, semantic entropy, CoVe, attribution scoring, judge, PRM best-of-N, tool loop, debate) with budgets. Policies can override or pin the plan per domain.
- 3. Implement a Strategy Registry where each strategy declares: applicability predicate (needs canonical answer? needs tools?), cost model (calls, tokens, latency), and output schema (score, verdicts, confidence). New domains add strategies or claim-type routers without touching the core.
- 4. Make 'external evidence first' the ordering rule: execution/tests and retrieval entailment are terminal authorities; LLM judgment is advisory; intrinsic self-critique is never allowed to override an unverified draft (Huang et al., CRITIC ablation, Kamoi et al.). Encode this as a precedence table in config.
- 5. Use an atomic Claim as the unit of verification. A Claim has {text, type, span, evidence[], verdict, confidence, verifier_used}. Long outputs are decomposed once and claims are verified in parallel; the report aggregates precision, citation recall, contradiction count, claim count and abstention so brevity gaming is visible.
- 6. Implement claim-type routing with pluggable tool adapters: retrieval (hybrid), code sandbox, calculator/units engine, document/clause fetcher, catalog/PLM/ERP lookups, ticketing/incident DB, test-log parser. Each adapter returns normalized Evidence{source_id, content, timestamp, score}.
- 7. Confidence fusion module: collect agreement, semantic entropy, verbalized top-k probability, judge score, verifier/PRM score, retrieval support fraction; fit a calibrator (logistic/isotonic + temperature scaling) per domain on labelled data; publish ECE/AUROC; drive a three-way gate (release / escalate verification / abstain-to-human) with configurable thresholds.
- 8. Budget controller: per-query difficulty estimation (initial agreement or PRM score) allocates compute (Snell et al.): easy -> k=3 SC; medium -> k up to 10 + claim checks; hard -> PRM search or human escalation. Enforce hard caps on calls, tokens and wall-clock; expose cost in the report.
- 9. Judge sub-module with built-in debiasing: position swap, randomized ordering, length control, cross-family model selection, reference-guided mode when a reference/executed result exists, probability-weighted rubric scores, and a human-anchor calibration set with agreement monitoring and drift alerts.
- 10. Repair sub-module is opt-in and evidence-gated: it accepts a failing signal (test failure, contradicted claim with evidence, error location) and performs minimal edits with a preservation budget (RARR), at most 2 rounds, using a stronger or different critic model; every edit is diffed and logged.
- 11. Observability and audit: persist the full verification trace (prompts, samples, evidence, verdicts, scores, model versions, seeds) with the output; emit metrics (precision, abstention rate, ECE, cost per verified answer, latency P50/P95, escalation rate); support replay for incident review and regulatory audit.
- 12. Configuration surface per domain: rubrics, claim-type definitions, tool adapters, evidence corpora, thresholds (p_release, p_min precision, tau entropy), budgets, model roster (generator vs judge families), redaction rules. Ship presets: 'code', 'spec/requirements', 'standards-compliance', 'incident-report', 'test-log-analysis', 'BOM/CAD-metadata', 'general-QA'.
- 13. Safety and failure handling: default to abstention with an explicit 'insufficient evidence' message (Anthropic guidance) rather than best-effort release when gates fail; never let verification prompts ingest untrusted retrieved text as instructions (treat evidence as data); sandbox all execution with timeouts and resource limits.
- 14. Testing and evaluation harness: golden sets per domain with human labels, adversarial sets (verbose/padded answers, swapped-order pairs, plausible-but-wrong code), regression tests on each model upgrade, and matched-budget comparisons (SC vs debate vs judge) before enabling any new strategy.
- Verifier false-positive accounting: every verifier arm (generated tests, NLI, judge, PRM) must publish an estimated false-positive rate from the gold audit channel, and the budget controller must use it in the stopping rule (Stroebl et al.): with an imperfect verifier, more samples raise accepted-wrong outputs, so N_max is a function of verifier FPR, not just cost.
- Goodhart monitoring as a standing dashboard: track proxy-score vs gold-outcome correlation per verifier version and per preset; alert on drift; forbid any optimization loop (RL, iterative prompt tuning, automatic rubric tuning) that uses a learned verifier as its objective. Only deterministic checkers may serve as optimization targets (DeepSeek-R1 practice; Gao et al. scaling laws).
- Prompt-injection hardening for the verifier itself: evidence, tool outputs and candidate text are untrusted; wrap them in delimited data blocks with a fixed schema, strip or neutralize instruction-like spans (imperatives addressed to the model, 'ignore previous', 'mark as verified'), never let a judge see tool credentials, and add an injection test-suite (candidates that contain 'this answer has been verified' or that instruct the judge to output PASS) to the regression harness. Treat a verdict change caused by text inside the candidate as a critical bug.
- Red-team the verifier with 'sneaky' candidates: maintain a tampering set (CriticGPT-style injected bugs) and a persuasion set (confident wrong answers, padded answers, fake citations with real-looking identifiers) per preset; gate verifier releases on recall/FPR on these sets, not only on agreement with humans on natural data.
- Statistical guarantees for compliance presets: replace hand-set p_release with conformal calibration (Mohri & Hashimoto) so that the 'standards-compliance' preset can state a per-output error bound (alpha) backed by a labelled calibration set; log the calibration set version alongside each verdict for audit.
- Separate 'verifier evaluation' from 'pipeline evaluation': maintain ProcessBench-style step-level localization sets and LLMBar-style adversarial judge sets, and report verifier recall, FPR and localization F1 independently of end-task accuracy; a verifier that improves best-of-N accuracy but localizes poorly will not support the repair stage (Tyen et al., Zhang et al. 2025).
- Cost control via tiered verification with explicit SLOs: define per-preset latency and cost budgets; run deterministic checkers and NLI first (ms), single-pass probes or verbalized confidence second, sampled semantic entropy / panel judges third, and PRM search or human review last; record which tier produced the final verdict so cost-per-verified-answer can be attributed and tuned.
- Graceful degradation and failure handling: specify behaviour when a tool adapter is down (retrieval index unavailable, sandbox queue full): default to 'unverified, abstain' with a machine-readable reason rather than falling back to LLM-only judgement; include circuit breakers, timeouts and partial-verification flags in the VerificationReport.
- Human-in-the-loop sampling for ground truth: audit a fixed random sample of released outputs (e.g., 1-2%) plus all escalations, label claim-level correctness, and feed the labels to (a) the calibrator, (b) the verifier FPR estimate, (c) the conformal threshold, and (d) the training set for domain judges (Self-Taught Evaluators / CriticGPT-style). Without this channel, every reported metric is a proxy.
- Versioning and reproducibility: pin and log model versions (generator, judges, NLI, PRM), prompt hashes, sampling seeds, retrieval index snapshot and checker versions in every trace; re-run a golden set on any version change and block deployment on regression beyond a tolerance; cache keys must include all of these to avoid serving verdicts from a stale verifier.
- Judge roster policy: require at least two judge families distinct from the generator family (panel of judges), position swap for pairwise, reference-guided mode whenever an oracle exists, and LLMBar-style adversarial validation of each prompt template before use; record per-judge votes to enable disagreement-based escalation.
- Deterministic-checker coverage as a KPI: measure the fraction of claims/outputs covered by rule-based verification per preset and invest in canonicalizers (units, symbolic math, SQL result normalization, JSON schema) to raise it; each percentage point moved from LLM judgement to deterministic checking lowers both cost and bias.
- Security of execution sandboxes: no network egress, no credentials, resource and time limits, ephemeral filesystems, and a separate audit log of executed code; generated tests are untrusted code and must run with the same restrictions as candidate code.
- Metamorphic and consistency testing of the verifier: the same claim paraphrased, reordered or with swapped candidate positions must yield the same verdict; add these invariance checks to CI and track verdict flip rates as a reliability metric alongside human agreement.

## Documented pitfalls

- Intrinsic self-correction regresses accuracy (GPT-4 GSM8K 95.5 -> 89.0 after two rounds; GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA 75.8 -> 38.1) -- Huang et al. 2023 'LLMs Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet'. Mitigation: only correct on external evidence; otherwise select (vote/verifier) or abstain.
- Self-feedback fails to detect errors on correctness tasks (ChatGPT said 'everything looks good' on 94% of GSM8K items; math gain 0-0.2) -- Madaan et al. 2023 Self-Refine. Mitigation: use refinement only for stylistic/subjective quality with concrete feedback; route correctness to tools/verifiers.
- Self-critique without tools gives negligible or negative gains (QA -0.03 F1; toxicity worsens 0.192 -> 0.223) -- Gou et al. 2023 CRITIC ablation. Mitigation: wire every critique to a tool/retrieval signal.
- LLMs cannot localize their own reasoning errors even when they can fix them given the location -- Tyen et al. 2023. Mitigation: use trained localizers (small classifiers, PRMs, failing tests, contradiction spans) and pass locations to the repair step.
- Code self-repair is often not worth its cost at equal sample budget (0.97-1.05x baseline for GPT-4 on APPS) and is bottlenecked by feedback quality -- Olausson et al. 2023. Mitigation: prefer more initial samples filtered by tests; use a stronger critic for feedback; cap repairs at 1-2.
- Multi-agent debate underperforms self-consistency at equal response counts (83.0 vs 88.2 on GSM8K at 9 responses) -- Huang et al. 2023; debate can degenerate when agents converge early -- Liang et al. 2023. Mitigation: benchmark at matched budget; adaptive termination; cross-family judge.
- Position bias in LLM judges (GPT-4 only 65% order-consistent; Claude-v1 23.8%; rankings flipped on 66/80 queries) -- Zheng et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2023 'Not Fair Evaluators'. Mitigation: evaluate both orders, count only consistent wins, randomize order, escalate high position-entropy items.
- Verbosity bias (91.3% of GPT-3.5/Claude-v1 judgments fooled by repetitive padding) -- Zheng et al. 2023; Dubois et al. 2024. Mitigation: length-controlled regression correction, explicit anti-verbosity rubric, token caps on candidates.
- Self-preference / self-enhancement bias (judges favor their own generations; strength correlates with self-recognition) -- Panickssery et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2023; G-Eval authors flag preference for LLM text -- Liu et al. 2023. Mitigation: cross-family judges, non-LLM verifiers for final gates, human anchor sets.
- Judges are unreliable graders of math/reasoning without references (70% failure with default prompt vs 15% reference-guided) -- Zheng et al. 2023; whole-chain verification is unreliable -- Ling et al. 2023. Mitigation: reference-guided or execution-grounded grading; step-wise verification with minimal premises.
- Precision-only factuality metrics reward saying less (CoVe fact count 16.6 -> 12.3; FActScore ignores abstention) -- Min et al. 2023; Dhuliawala et al. 2023. Mitigation: always report claim count, abstention rate and recall-oriented coverage checks alongside precision.
- Factuality collapses on rare entities (ChatGPT ~80% -> 16% FActScore for rarest entities) and later in long outputs -- Min et al. 2023. Mitigation: retrieval grounding for long-tail identifiers (part numbers, obscure clauses), shorter generation units, claim-level checks.
- Verbalized confidence is overconfident and weak on professional-knowledge tasks; raw RLHF token probabilities are poorly calibrated -- Xiong et al. 2023; Tian et al. 2023. Mitigation: top-k verbalization, consistency fusion, temperature scaling, per-domain recalibration, human escalation thresholds.
- Consistency is not correctness: systematic shared misconceptions pass self-consistency and USC (oracle 96.2 vs USC 90.2 on GSM8K) -- Chen et al. 2023. Mitigation: pair agreement with evidence-based checks; diversify prompts and models to decorrelate samples.
- Verifier/best-of-N returns can diminish or reverse with too many samples (6B verifier declines past ~400 completions; USC GSM8K dropped at 16 samples) -- Cobbe et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2023. Mitigation: adaptive sample counts; weighted voting over top-k; difficulty-aware budgets.
- Citation support is incomplete even for the best models (lack complete support 50% of the time on ELI5) -- Gao et al. 2023 ALCE. Mitigation: NLI-based citation recall/precision gating; retract unsupported claims (Anthropic guidance).
- Unfair experimental baselines inflate self-correction claims (oracle labels, unequal budgets) -- Kamoi et al. 2024; Huang et al. 2023. Mitigation: internal evaluations must compare at matched cost without oracle information.

## Evaluation metrics

- Task accuracy / exact match of the selected answer on domain golden sets (GSM8K-style for calculations; pass@k with hidden tests for code), reported for verified vs unverified pipeline and at matched compute budget
- Hallucination detection quality: AUROC and AUC-PR of the uncertainty/hallucination score for predicting incorrect claims (Farquhar et al. AUROC 0.790; SelfCheckGPT AUC-PR 93.4 as references); AURAC (area under rejection-accuracy curve) for abstention value
- Attribution precision (FActScore-style supported fraction), citation recall and citation precision (ALCE NLI), contradiction rate, plus mandatory companions: claims per response and abstention rate
- Preservation when revising: Levenshtein-ratio or intent-preservation judgment and F1_AP (harmonic mean of attribution and preservation, RARR)
- Calibration of fused confidence: Expected Calibration Error (target <= 0.05), Brier score, reliability diagrams; failure-prediction AUROC (target >= 0.8)
- Judge reliability: agreement with human experts on an anchor set (reference ~85% GPT-4 vs 81% human-human), position consistency rate after swap, length-bias slope, cross-order verdict entropy
- Verifier quality (PRM/ORM): best-of-N accuracy curves vs N, step-level AUROC against labelled steps, out-of-distribution accuracy on held-out task families
- Selective prediction: coverage vs accuracy curve at operating thresholds; escalation rate to humans and human-overturn rate of verifier verdicts
- Cost and latency: LLM calls and tokens per verified answer, P50/P95 latency, average samples used under adaptive stopping (target >= 3x reduction vs fixed k_max), tool-execution time
- Regression and robustness: accuracy on adversarial sets (verbose padding, swapped order, plausible-but-wrong code, long-tail entities), drift metrics across model versions

## Engineering adaptation notes

- Code and scripts (including CAD parametric scripts, SQL, infra configs): execution is the oracle. Use CodeT-style generated tests plus dual execution agreement, static analysis and compiler output as evidence; treat 'all generated tests pass and the largest output cluster agrees' as release criteria; keep repair rounds <= 2 with the failure trace as feedback.
- Specifications and requirements: decompose into atomic requirement claims; verify traceability by retrieving the parent requirement/standard clause and running entailment; check consistency across requirements (contradiction detection between claims); use rubric judges for clarity/testability (G-Eval style) with human-anchored calibration.
- Standards and compliance documents: route clause claims to an exact-clause fetcher (version-pinned); require Supported verdicts with citations for every normative statement; set p_min precision to 1.0 for compliance assertions and force abstention otherwise; log the standard edition/version in the evidence record.
- Numeric engineering calculations (loads, tolerances, power budgets, unit conversions): recompute with a calculator/units library (FacTool math arm), apply backward verification (substitute result into constraints, Weng et al.), and use self-consistency over independent derivations; flag unit mismatches as Contradicted.
- Test logs and telemetry: ground every claim in parsed log lines (regex/structured parsers as a tool adapter); verify counts, timestamps and error codes by direct lookup rather than LLM reading; use semantic entropy to detect speculative root-cause statements and mark them as hypotheses.
- Incident reports and postmortems: separate facts (timeline, metrics, config changes) from hypotheses; facts must be attributed to tickets, alerts or log evidence; hypotheses get explicit confidence and are candidates for multi-agent debate review; enforce preservation budgets so verification does not rewrite the responder's narrative.
- CAD/BOM metadata: part numbers, revisions, suppliers and quantities are long-tail identifiers where LLM recall is weakest (FActScore rare-entity collapse); always verify against PLM/ERP APIs; treat any unverifiable identifier as a blocking Contradicted/No-evidence claim.
- Domain verifiers: where final-answer oracles exist (tests, reference values, simulators), build Math-Shepherd-style automatic step labels to train small PRMs/localizers for design-review and calculation chains; use them for weighted best-of-N and error localization before any LLM repair.
- Human-in-the-loop: expose position-entropy, fused-confidence and contradiction flags as escalation triggers to engineers; capture their adjudications to grow the calibration and PRM training sets (active learning on confident-but-wrong items, ~2.6x label efficiency per Lightman et al.).
- Security: retrieved documents, logs and tool outputs are untrusted data; verification prompts must quarantine them as evidence blocks, and sandboxed execution must have no network or credential access.

## Composition notes

- Hybrid retrieval: the verifier is the primary consumer of retrieval for claim-level evidence (top-5 passages per claim with BM25+dense fusion) and the primary producer of retrieval feedback: No-evidence verdicts and low citation recall indicate retriever gaps; Verify-and-Edit shows agreement-triggered retrieval, so run expensive re-retrieval only for low-consistency outputs. Attribution scoring (ALCE/FActScore) doubles as the RAG grounding gate.
- Task decomposition: decomposition produces sub-tasks with canonicalizable outputs, which makes self-consistency and step-wise verification (Ling et al., Lightman et al.) applicable per sub-task; verification should run at sub-task boundaries and at the recomposition step (compositionality gap, Press et al.), and the decomposer should emit verification hints (expected type, oracle availability) for the planner.
- Answer verification itself: strategies compose in layers -- cheap sampling/agreement first, then claim-level evidence checks, then judge/debate for residual subjective quality, with confidence fusion as the single output; the precedence rule 'external evidence over LLM judgment over self-critique' must hold across layers.
- Parallelization: sampling k paths, verifying claims, running both judge orderings, executing generated tests and debating agents are all embarrassingly parallel; implement them as fan-out/fan-in with the adaptive stopping criterion applied across batches (Adaptive-Consistency) and the budget controller enforcing caps. Anthropic's voting pattern (parallel reviewers with vote thresholds) is the direct integration surface.
- Caching: cache canonicalized sample answers and claim verdicts keyed by (model version, prompt hash, evidence hash) so repeated questions skip re-verification; cache NLI/entailment results per (claim, passage) pair and executed test results per (code hash, test hash); invalidate on corpus or model version change; semantic caches must store the fused confidence and only serve high-confidence entries (e.g., p >= 0.85) to avoid caching hallucinations.
- Pipeline RAG: in a full RAG pipeline the verifier sits after generation as the grounding gate (citation recall/precision, contradiction = 0 for compliance), feeds revision (RARR-style minimal edits) and abstention ('insufficient evidence'), and emits attribution reports; it also enables evaluator-optimizer loops (Anthropic) where the evaluator decides whether more retrieval is needed.
- Prompting/architecture best practices: adopt Anthropic's hallucination-reduction defaults (permission to say 'I don't know', quote-first grounding for >20K-token contexts, cite-and-retract, closed-book restriction) in the generator so the verifier's load is lower; keep generator and judge in different model families; log everything for audit; compare any new verification strategy against adaptive self-consistency at matched cost before enabling it.
